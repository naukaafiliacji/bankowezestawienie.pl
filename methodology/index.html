Research methodology

How we build our bank rankings.

BankRanking compares consumer banking, saving and investment-related services across Europe using a structured research process. The goal is not to create a mathematically perfect score. It is to make the differences between major providers easier to understand and to explain why one option may fit a particular customer better than another.

Four rules shape every ranking
  • Official sources come first.
  • Commercial relationships do not decide ranking position.
  • Different product categories use different comparison factors.
  • Every ranking is a dated research snapshot, not a permanent verdict.

What a BankRanking position means

A ranking position is an editorial comparison of the providers included in a specific shortlist. It is not a claim that a bank is universally “better” for every customer, and it is not an exhaustive register of every licensed institution in a country.

01 · Method in brief

Seven steps from market research to ranking.

We begin with the market, not with affiliate availability. The research process identifies the providers that matter to consumers, collects comparable product information, checks the data against primary sources and then evaluates the products using category-specific factors.

1Define the marketCountry, customer segment and product category are fixed before providers are compared.
2Select relevant providersMajor domestic banks, large banking groups and widely used digital providers form the initial shortlist.
3Collect source dataFees, eligibility, promotions, access rules and product features are taken primarily from official sources.
4Normalise differencesDifferent pricing structures and conditions are translated into comparable consumer-facing fields.
5Compare category factorsThe factors that matter for a personal account are not treated as identical to those for a savings or investment product.
6Editorial reviewWe check whether the resulting order makes sense in the context of the local market and the intended customer.
7Publish a dated snapshotRankings show a research date because rates, promotions, fees and eligibility can change.
02 · Coverage

We rank major consumer-relevant providers, not every licence holder.

Some European banking markets contain dozens or hundreds of licensed institutions. Including every private bank, specialist lender, mortgage-only institution or small local entity would make a consumer ranking less useful rather than more complete.

Our country shortlists therefore focus on banks and providers with meaningful retail relevance: large market presence, broad consumer availability, significant local usage, a substantial branch or digital footprint, or a clear role in the category being compared.

Digital banks are treated by actual consumer relevance. A provider does not need a traditional nationwide branch network to enter a shortlist if it has become widely used in that country.
03 · Evidence

A source hierarchy, not a pile of search results.

When sources disagree, the closer the source is to the actual product or regulator, the more weight it receives. Secondary sources can help identify a change or provide context, but they should not override current official product documentation.

1
Official product sources

Bank product pages, tariff tables, terms and conditions, fee documents, eligibility rules and official promotion pages.

2
Regulators & central banks

Supervisory registers, deposit-protection information, market statistics and official information on regulated institutions.

3
Bank-group disclosures

Annual reports, investor materials and official corporate announcements used for scale, ownership and structural changes.

4
Reliable secondary context

Established financial media and specialist sources may be used to cross-check context, but not as the sole source for current fees or product terms.

05 · Age-specific banking

Why 18–26 and 26+ are separate.

A young customer can face a materially different price and product proposition from an older customer at the same bank. Student eligibility, age-based fee waivers and youth promotions can change which account is the most attractive.

18–26

More emphasis on youth eligibility, student or young-adult fee waivers, travel usability, mobile banking and age-limited bonuses.

26+

Standard adult pricing, recurring long-term costs and ordinary eligibility become more important once youth exemptions disappear.

06 · Promotions

A large bonus can improve an offer. It does not automatically win the ranking.

Welcome bonuses, cashback and limited-time campaigns are recorded separately from the long-term account proposition. We look at the size of the benefit together with its conditions: required card spend, salary payments, minimum activity, age restrictions, deadlines and how long the customer must keep the account.

Promotional sorting is deliberately separate. Users can sort for a high listed bonus when that is what they care about, while the default ranking can still consider the account beyond the first few months.
07 · Investing

Banks first, specialist market second.

BankRanking is primarily a banking portal. For investment-related rankings, banks and banking groups are therefore the default comparison view. Dedicated brokers, robo-advisers and specialist investment platforms remain useful, but they are shown as a separate specialist market so the user can understand that they are comparing a different type of provider.

08 · Ranking presentation

Why you do not see “9.7/10”.

Consumer finance comparisons contain judgement calls and rapidly changing data. Displaying tiny score differences can imply a level of scientific precision that the underlying decision does not support.

9.7/10
We prefer explainable differences to pseudo-precision.

Structured internal ordering can help organise a shortlist, but the public ranking focuses on the reasons that matter: costs, eligibility, product conditions, strengths and trade-offs.

09 · Editorial independence

Commercial relationships and rankings are separate questions.

BankRanking may use affiliate links or work with affiliate networks. A commercial relationship can affect whether BankRanking earns revenue after a click or successful application; it should not determine which provider is included, what factual product information is displayed or where a provider is placed in the default editorial ranking.

No pay-to-rank

A higher commission is not a ranking criterion.

Sponsored content is labelled

Advertising should be distinguishable from editorial comparison.

Official facts remain official facts

Commercial status does not change fees, conditions or eligibility data.

10 · Freshness & corrections

A ranking is a research snapshot.

Banking products can change quickly, especially promotions, interest rates and fee waivers. Structural facts such as a bank’s ownership or market presence usually change more slowly. We therefore treat different data fields according to how quickly they can become stale.

PromotionsDeadline, reward amount and qualification conditions are time-sensitive and should be checked against the bank’s current offer before applying.
Fees & ratesChecked against current product pages or tariff documents when a ranking is researched or materially updated.
Provider coverageReviewed when market structure changes, banks merge, rebrand, exit a market or a digital provider becomes materially relevant.
CorrectionsIf a factual error is identified, the source should be re-checked and the comparison corrected rather than defended simply because it was previously published.
